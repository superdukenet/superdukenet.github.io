this is not my '69 440 6BBL (more commonly known as the "SIX PACK" for the 3 x 2BBL Holley carbs) road runner but it is one of three known to have been painted from the factory in gold (Spanish Gold Y4 paint code). This one is a hardtop (like the GTX above) and the rear windows roll down, and the other gold car is also a hardtop, where my car is a coupe and has a "B" post and flip out rear quarter windows. They only made 1432 of these cars in the Spring of 1969. I'm in the process of restoring my A12 car right now but I keep getting side tracked with my Super Duke lately Oh the hood is fiberglass and there are no hood hinges and it is held in place with four hood pins, you have to lift the hood off and usually you place it on the roof or lay it on the ground for someone to step on . This is the only American production car to ever come without hood hinges. The A12 package included a 440 cu. in. engine that was not available before this in a road runner, an Edelbrock aluminium intake manifold, 3 x 2BBL Holley carburetors, a max cooling system, Hemi suspension, a 4.10 Dana, and black steel wheels without hub caps or wheel covers. You couldn't order or get A/C or cruise control or a towing package...a bare bones, bad @$$ poster child of the muscle car era IMO. In one magazine test where they pitted magazine editors against a pro drag racer Ronnie Sox the car with Sox driving went 12.91 @ 111 mph stock with stock tyres. The only cars that were slightly quicker back then were a '67 AC bodied 427 Cobra and a '67 427 Vette......but A12 owners consider those two-seat sports cars not 4,000+ lb muscle cars
