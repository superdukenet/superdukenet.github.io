So fitted the camera and screen today, (just used the suction cup mount stuck to the rev counter), and went for a test ride. Wow, much better than I expected. In fact I'm a convert - this is the future. did every sort of road, town traffic, filtering, bumpy country lanes, fast A roads and a bit of motorway. Wide angle lens is just about perfect - I can just see the two Akras at the very edge of the screen and the view at the edge is very similar to what you'd see in the tip of your mirrors. Definitely wide enough to see a car just to the offside and rear - where you'd want to check as a life saver before overtaking. But the view directly behind is a revelation - not something I've ever experienced on a bike before - just like a car rear view.

Tried riding into the sun which is the best camera picture, (good colour and exposure), and it's OK to see the screen. Tried riding away from setting sun and as expected this was the worst for the exposure but it was OK and no worse than looking into a real mirror riding away from a setting sun. There's no vibration issues or blurring - which was what I expected.

Post 18 by bic_bicknell, 08 Nov 2011, 06:18

#18
It's easy mate. But for this one-off I will not print as it would not be cost effective. This is how I do it in the modeling world of product design.

You have to visualise the design in layers - but in reverse of the way you normally think of them. On top is the clear sheet material. Then first level down is the black and white lettering. then the orange lines and blocking, then finally the black background. Instead of screen printing I use dry rubdown transfers. I make an artwork for each of the colours, (in Illustrator), and then I send them to one of the many companies that specialise in packaging mock-ups - this is the one I use. http://www.main-titles.co.uk/

Remember the most important thing is to get them to either put the rubdown glue surface over the TOP of the colour or make your artwork in REVERSE. Other wise your design and type will be the wrong way round. Specify the colours from a Pantone book. 24 hour turn around usually.

Then you carefully apply each layer of colour onto the back of the Perspex. It helps if you have made some registration marks on each of the artworks which helps a lot in getting the registration correct. You do not have many chances at repositioning these transfers and small type can easily get stuck and come off in the wrong position. Always get a spare set of each sheet. If it goes wrong then lighter fluid will get everything off again.

When all the transfers are down then, (on this particular screen design), I will have to mask off the places where the screen and gear indicator are placed and I want to remain clear. When this is done the last thing is to spray the whole back surface with black paint. Don't over do the paint as it may start to dissolve the transfer material - just build up the layers. Doesn't matter what finish the paint surface is - what you're trying to do is to seal on the transfers. When you turn it over and see the paint finish through the Perspex it will be amazing and perfect and shiny. Like an i phone. And all the layers of transfers will be miraculously sealed between the black paint and the bottom of the Perspex. The depth of the Persex has a strange effect on the type and design - the thicker it is the more this effect. It's a bit three dimensional - looks good.

This is when you realise there is a letter missing from one of the words or there is a small fly also captured under the paint. Time to start again

Not sure about the "roll" indicator marks yet! Will think about it as the project develops - maybe a bit too gimmicky for me, like the wear marks on Metzler M5 tyre edges. Couldn't resist seeing what it looked like though.

#41
Ha ha! Non of you will want to know the cost of this.

Ask any of the forum guys who develop stuff, Aaron doing his Motohooligan stuff and Mr Z32 and his dual velocity stacks. I'm sure they will tell the same story, it's not the price of the end part, it's the time and effort and cost of trial and error that goes into the front end of anything. But this is my profession and so I'm used to the cost of developing things. This unit was done on an absolute shoestring - because I was paying for it. But if it was a real job, with a real client I would have spent a lot more!!

But seriously. The cost of this is not economically viable. Otherwise we would have seen digital mirrors on production bikes years ago.

So for those of you who want to know.

MATERIALS
1. Blue tooth wide angle lens camera and original screen were bought at a unit from 4kam.com, it's really a reversing camera I think, pretty good resolution and function. About £140
2. Healtech gear indicator unit was about the same if I remember £130
3. Frame component CNCd out of black nylon and post finished. (Excluding CAD Pro-E work and file output for rapid prototyping) - this was done as a favour at half price by one of my suppliers. £200
4. Front and back screens CNCd from 3mm acrylc £50 (as above)
5.Rub-down transfers in orange and black from Maintitles in London £60
6. Universal mounting bracket from a US supplier. About £60 but with shipping and taxes it was over £100 (outrageous!!! The worst part of this undertaking )
7. 1 metre of 2mm diameter rubber 'O'ring material for seals from Pentonville rubber in Islington. £2.50
8. Assortment of stainless screws, nuts and washers £20
9. Shrink sleeve tubing for the electrical connections £2.50
9. Black spray paint. £6

TIME
Don't want to even think about it

Working in the product design business though, I know what things should cost if they ever actually got produced for a mass market. This could be yours for the less than the original price I paid for the camera unit. But as you can see the cost of making "one offs" is pretty high and I don't expect anyone would be interested at the above cost.

But I get to ride about with my, more or less, unique digital mirrors and enjoy a free rear view and not seeing my elbows all the time!!

And have fun with my "Leanometer" or "Kneedownometer" or "Bancometer" haven't decided what to call it yet.
