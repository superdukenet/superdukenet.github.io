Hey fellas,

Well, the day has finally come where I have decided to sell my beloved SDR.

As you know I'm a 6x KTM owner, and on my second superduke and third LC8-engined bike. I love these machines, and I love the KTM brand... but I've been hanging on to this bike for all the wrong reasons, almost out of nostalgia and not being able to bring myself to leave the Superduke fold. It's such a great bike to own, even if you're not on it. LOL.

Reasons are complex, but basically my riding is changing.

The SDR is the best bike for the old me. Aggressive, loud, in a fooking hurry, and grinning like the devil on PCP... but as time goes by I'm becoming less angry and prefer to ride smooth instead of fast. As some of you noticed, I picked up a (cough, splutter) for slow summer rides (85-bhp at the wheel, LOL) and to my horror, I've discovered that not only am I faster round Brands Hatch on it, but that it's also better through town, and it's even easier and less demanding to ride as an every day bike, despite being an air-cooled, twin-shock retro roadster. It corners faster too.

This isn't about the bike tho, it's about me. When faced with both sets of keys I opt for jeans, piss-pot lid and the Ducati keys. Also, I'm 45, with 2 kids, and my biking tastes are becoming more about lifestyle than the latest power commander, TuneECU, or which widget will give me another 3 bhp. I want timeless biking, not this year's latest bike. I don't really care about a 1200 SDR, and that's weird.

Also, the Dutchess was knocked off her Buell a few weeks ago, and though she's on the mend, it reminded more how mortal we all are, and while any bike is dangerous, I'm well aware that the super duke has always made me ride like a cnut. Time to chill a little, I think, and you just can't chill on an SDR.


So... I still love my Superduke, and if I was a little more financially stable I'd keep it, just to hang on to a modern classic, but it's crazy to hang on to such a high spec machine as a "second bike" while I spend my bike-money on an old cafe racer.

I'm not in a rush to get rid of it and I want the right price (the mods and spec are ridiculous), so as this isn't the best time of year to sell, I may still have it through winter. ...and if you'll let me, I'd love to hang out here still, and keep meeting up with the mates I've made on road and track.


One final comment: Out of all the biker-types out there, Superduke riders are the maddest, quickest, and most opinionated, individualistic, loonies I have ever met.
So many good riders, so many decent fellas, so much knowledge, trust, passion...
I'm more distressed about leaving a brotherhood of like-minded nutters than I am about losing the bike. It's a very exclusive, very special club. I will definitely have regrets, and I can barely bring myself to press the Submit button.

Thanks for all the replies guys.I really appreciate your comments, and yes, as for my mates, those are always gonna be there. I'm still on two wheels after all.

It's not so much an age thing. At 45 I feel pretty young for my years and I intend to keep riding for as many years as possible, it's more about the fact that a new type of biking has come onto my radar, that is even more about the lifestyle, and ticks boxes that one-model and one-make can't tick. I've also lost my passion for outright bhp, and gained a new passion for lightness and handling. The Superduke is a big bike (ok, I'm only 5'9") and while it's brilliantly flickable in a SuperMoto kinda way, it's limited.

I've also lost interest in owning the newest thing out there. The Tuono leaves me cold, as does any bike that needs rider-aids to keep it upright. It's like the Eurofighter - turn off the computers and it's not even aerodynamic. Most modern bikes bore me to tears. I'd rather own something bespoke; fast, light and basic with timeless design and no sell-by date - where I don't have to get the latest model to be relevant.

If the Ducati wasn't such a good bike I would NEED the SDR too, but since it's surprisingly good and practical it's made the SDR slightly redundant. If the Duc had been slower - or if the SDR was a bit smoother and more practical - then I'd probably have room to keep both bikes in my stable.

For anyone who likes the cafe racer vibe, but doesn't fancy a lardy old cement mixer like the Thruxton, or the stunning but overpriced Norton, the Ducati Sport Classic 1000 is about 179kg (...that's Italian kg, dry) and extremely flickable, with Brembo brakes and decent suspension (ok, it needs new springs - but so did my SDR) even the rear twin adjustable Marzzochis are pretty good. It's like a 1000cc Monster with Clipons and more style.

When I did my first trackday at Brands on the Duc I made the mistake of entering in the Novice group (slow and retro, right?). To my surprise (and I'm not exaggerating) I lapped everyone on every session, except my bro on his K1300R - who I still easily passed. Ok, they were crap slow townies on bikes they couldn't ride, but you get my drift. (If you've ridden with me you know I'm probably Mr Average, so it was the bike, not me.)



Anyway, the SDRs not gone yet.... Let's see if anyone is even buying bikes at this time of year!

All this lament, lust, mourning and what have you ... too bad you can't put a price tag on any of it, and sell it.

Other than the shitty situation of it being a buyer's market at the moment, I'm glad for you that you're selling your bike. You read that right: glad.

Not so much glad that you'll no longer own it (not personal), as that there will be a new guy (let's be honest, statistically, it will be a guy) who will be super stoked to own it, and thrash it just as you once did. He'll set it up to his liking, buy accessories for it, and hopefully even see some track time with it.

Think of it as the rebirth of a bike. A bike meant to be ridden.

This IS what Café was, and is all about. The "streetfighter" thing is a myth, just another label - more contemporary perhaps - for "Café". Its something you can't just buy - its only something you can make. It looks like you took your SDR in a certain direction, and the next owner is likely to change things. Its a good thing. The bike will get the energy it deserves. It will make someone happy.

Garage queen and "show" bikes are pointless. You want art? Go to a museum. If you can afford to collect it - more power to you. But Motorcycles are for riding. And riding mercilessly. Even the best ones are relatively inexpensive (you all know what a comparably performing car costs ... ), and when in the right hands, they really go. Sure there's risk involved, and not just to the rider. When you don't want to do that any longer on a particular bike - regardless the reason - its good to give it up. Otherwise, you're just throwing money away, and not getting the joy from doing so.

The real bike guy owns it all, not just the wheezy nostalgia-trip that doesn't leak as badly or require the maintenance of the real-vintage-deal. He's got his hooligan toy, a track whore, and the retro/vintage/cafe pig-of-yore. He might even own a "sport tourer", you know, something that not only turns well, but has a fairing, bags, and all that crap.

Again, I'm glad you're selling your SDR, and I'm glad you're enjoying whatever it is you're riding more of now.

BTW - for you queer on the ridiculously expensive Supermonos, there are folks (including a local guy) who are building similar bikes (single conversion with con-rod balancer) out of 848s, 1098s, and 1198s ... to get in to certain race classes. Just sayin' - you can too.
