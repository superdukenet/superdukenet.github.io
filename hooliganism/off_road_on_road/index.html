So, I've had my EXC for a coupla months now...I'd done a few days off road before that, 2 of which were in Spain. Basically I reckon I have about 30 hours experience off road. and I fucken love it!! I'm not that great at it but I dont care, it really gets me out of my comfort zone, I get to see wicked parts of the countryside and rip the shit out of it with a bunch of top blokes.

My point is....I have more fun at 40mph off road than 140mph on it. It's safer too. I mean I know I crash a LOT more off road but I'm more likely to land at 15-40mph on mud or grass in full armour rather than on a fast corner at 60-110mph either lowsiding or highsiding on tarmac, possibly being hit by another vehicle, getting nicked, going to prison (for the kind of speeds I can do if I so desired), losing my licence and my job and maybe my life.

Basically, the more i think about it, the more i think of selling the beloved SDR and keeping it muddy from now on. It's crazy how much i love it compared to road riding when you look at how much I have done of each.

I'd love to keep the R...she is an amazing bike....SO focused and almost perfect for me in every way....but maybe that money could be put to better use? Maybe trade her in for an SM for some slower speed fun round tighter twisties at more copper friendly speeds? I would love to know how the EXC would behave on SM wheels.....but then I just think, why not grab a 690 SM or a DukeIII...


Anyway, I'm interested in your thoughts...

Need Vs Want welcome to my world. I take it you no longer use a bike to commute?

I am having the same debate though not because of the off roading which hasn’t gripped me yet (17hrs to your 30) That said, I am looking forward to enduroland / motocross tracks and a summer of dry tracks to hoon about on.

Riding thorugh shitty deep mud is no fun at all at my level of experience, neither is cleaning the bike after.

I have also questioned the need for a road bike to use purely for enjoyment (i.e far in excess of the speed limit) in the current climate of unmarked bikes and telephoto cop cameras looking to obtain a ban plus a big fine and trial by Youtube. I have reduced my road riding from 10k plus per year down to about 2k. I need my licence for work. I don’t have any fun riding at legal speeds so that isn’t an option.

Trackdays have become another pain in the arse. Since 2005/6/7 when I did about 10 per year I have decreased my attendance to about 2 per year. Mostly due to the whinging briefings and protracted noise testing. Who wants to lose money for a day off work for, at best , 7 sessions with at least 3 foreshortened by unnecessary red flags? So it is a no brainer, the ZX636 B1H that hasn’t turned a wheel since last May ought to go on e bay.

However, I still like to do a couple of Lyddens per year, plus a few spontaneous road rides so I do need a road bike. The only thing I would willingly swap my SDR for would be an earlier 2005/6 SD if I needed the money.

Insuring 2 bikes isn’t much dearer than insuring one. Tax on an off roader is peanuts. Unless you have to get rid I would say stay as you are. A 690 is neither, one thing, or the other and would be found wanting on or off road. In future years you may need to get a cheaper road and off road bike but I would still say it is worth having two separate bikes rather than a jack of all trades master of none. Likewise a 450EXC with road wheels will be a boring, gutless, uncomfortable bike that cant keep up with anyone you know…except Freestyler .

Unless you desperately need the money or are certain that you wont ride on roads again stay as you are. Whatever you do, don’t buy a mid life crisis bike that was shit in your youth just because you remember the big boys had one (sorry thought you were Schnauzer for a minute)

I decided over the winter that I was going all dirt this year. With a new kid I just don't have the time for track days and dirt riding. And I haven't put in any decent road riding in three years (commuting blows in the big city and I have to ride 80mi's to find two consecutive curves and then 80 more to find another two). Trying to keep up with parts, tyres, maintenance, insurance of two bikes is time consuming and expensive. In my single days no problem. Time and money was all mine. All that droning out of the way now I can say I already miss track days and the season is just getting started here. Technically I haven't even missed a track day yet!

Dirt riding is a kick in the ass no doubt! You get to do all the things you wish you could do on a street bike with 1/1000th of the consequences. Slide it, wheelie it, go as fast as your balls allow you, drop it, flip it, jump it, drop it, flip it, pick it up and keep going. I crash more and get hurt more in one hour of dirt than I ever did on a street bike but (for the most part) it's the kind of hurt you get up and ride away from. My riding season starts two months earlier and goes a month longer for dirt. But really it's not a street vs. dirt thing for me. I kinda get a pit in my stomach when I think of giving up the track and a street bike and particularly my SD. But, for now, I really needed to pick one and dirt is offering more riding to me. My plan is to keep my 250xcw for enduro and hare scrambles and get a full on MX bike for track riding.

Like someone mentioned, if the money tied up in the SDR isn't a problem just hold it for a year and get your dirt fix guilt free. The SDR will be there ready for some love when you quit cheating on it

#33
Buy a 690 enduro and supermoto wheels, it works great both on and off road.

I brought my 690 enduro R converted to 690SMC (forks, brakes, wheels++) as a back-up for road racing practise (so not track day) last weekend. Unfortunately SDR fuel pump broke so had to use the 690 for the entire 2 days. And I'm genuinely shocked A single shouldn't be this fast Of course I loose on the straights, but not nearly as much as you would think and it pulls really strong out of the corners. Took a couple of sessions before finding a ok body position basically not hanging that much off. I was in the middle of the intermediate group and I was the only guy with plates (that was off for the occasion) and street legal tyres, the rest was 600s and mostly 1000/1200 sports bikes on slicks. And I'm no Rossi.

Being a supermoto it of course turns very quick but a 600 isn't exactly slow in turns either so it's the motor that was the big, big surprise I was going to keep the Superduke as it obviously is better on the road and track. But not sure about the first and though probably maybe 1-2 seconds slower on a track, I think I had more fun on the 690 And it's good fun passing sportsbikes with a big cross bike on 17" wheels And much cheaper to crash so you don't worry that much

I've considered the superduke a keeper, a bike I would never sell and tell my grand children this was before ABS and traction control was normal and you had to control the bike yourself. But not sure any more, seriously considering selling it And I really love this bike it breaks my heart even thinking about it

So the 690 actually works for anything, and is GOOD at anything, Race track, Supermoto track, twisty roads, touring, gravel roads, Off road (Myself I'm rubbish...but talking about the bike here).

It's not a motorway bike of course, but I stay away from motoways and any straight roads anyway. And don't think it would be a big problem, go 120kph instead of 160kph. Then it's the 12L gas tank, but actually lasted about the same track time as the 18.5L superduke. And there is plenty of options expanding to twice that and more. It is big though, especailly the R I've got (but works best for converting to SMC), so in rough terrain it's a handful for a beginner (like me). Not that heavy mind 130kg with slip on without gas.

Of course you need to change wheels to make this work, but easy job. For track also changing to the SMC forks and front brakes is a definate improvement. But not that big deal. 4 bolts to loosen a fork leg and 2 bolts for the brake lever. The calliper must off when changing wheels anyway. Have complete sets so no need to bleed the brakes.
