2nd gear about 4k rpm roughly 40mph,pull in the clutch give it a bit more throttle as you feed the clutch out at the same time giving it a gentle tug on the bar's. Your bike should gentley beggin to rise as your speed builds,control it on the throttle and you'll be able feel the balance point as it goes really light...."warning" it's best not to go beyond this point.

It all sounds simple,and it is a good throttle hand and a bit of confidence and loads of practice and you'll master it. It does'nt have to be a violent procedure,but if you prefer to dump the clutch instead of feeding it out be warned that on cold tyre's you'll just spin up,so expect some sideways movement.

I'm definately no wheelie pro but i'm willing to show anyone on ride out's how i do it. Exiting roundabouts it's just gotta be done onlooker's no doubt think,look at that twat but i'm doing it for myself as i enjoy it

Said I would post an update on my previous post after another ride which I managed on Wednesday so here goes. I had never really thought about it a lot before but I paid attention this time and this is what I found easiest and most fun.

This is my first twin for a long time and it does make a difference to the feel compared to a four but essentialy everything works the same way.

Get to something like 3 - 4K in second, pull in the clutch just enough for it to slip, hammer the throttle and as the revs pass 7Kish let the clutch back out quickly. The wheel will float up, if you freak at any stage shutting the throttle slams it back down pronto. Forget the back brake, not necessary on this twin and just one more thing to worry about when you are already trying to get used to the new technique.

Things to ensure before doing this. Sit upright on the bike and make sure you are not hanging off one side at all as this will make the bike lean which you don't want. relax your arms, don't pull at the bars and don't lean forward or back. In fact the less you do the better the bike will behave.

First few times it may not work and the probable causes will be too shy with the throttle or to slow letting the clutch back in. Some people have said feed it back in but I more or less dump it and that seems to work well. Its like riding over bumps on dirt, the faster you go at them the smoother it feels.

Once you have done a few try starting with you foot under the gear lever. Apply a little upwards pressure and the moment you relax the throttle it will change into third with the wheel still up but probably on the way down. Full throttle makes for a smooth landing

I'm no expert but this worked for me on the SD. happy to answer any questions and also if you watch the vid posted in this thread you can see it working in action. He is much better than me!!
