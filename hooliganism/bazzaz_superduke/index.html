#1
I picked up my bike from Bazzaz this afternoon. Of course, as soon as I donned my gear to ride it, it started to rain. Anyone in Southern California knows that the absolute worst time to ride is following a brief storm. The roads become as slippery as ice, so its maiden voyage will have to wait until tomorrow.

The good news:

As expected, my bike, with the stock airbox, DNA filter, and KTM Akrapovic map was running "a bit" rich, ha ha. Bazzaz dyno tuned the bike using its Z-AFM device. She showed 5 HP and 4 lb ft gains. The air fuel mixture is also much smoother.





The automapping unit is beyond cool. Basically, once you have the Z-AFM unit, it constantly monitors your A/F mixture and builds a map based upon your riding conditions (altitude, temp, etc.) Once the data is built up, you can import it into one of two map locations within the unit to fine tune your map. It seemed to be very user friendly. If I switch to a Motohooligan Box, it would be a simple matter of riding the bike to create a new map for it.

The bad news:

For the traction control and quick shifter to work, the Bazzaz system requires a means to track engine rpm or rear wheel speed in each gear. The GPS on the SD only reads gears 1-3 and there was no good location to mount a separate wheel speed sensor on the SD as there is on the RC8. Also, because there is not a great market for SD aftermarket parts in the US, Bazzaz has no present plans to construct a bracket to mount a wheel speed sensor on the SD. So, for now, there will be no TC or QS for our beloved SD.

I know of one SD in the country (DIno John?) that has an aftermarket rear brake bracket that would probably serve as a proper perch for the Bazzaz wheel speed sensor, but that is a one off prototype that, I'm told, will never see production. If someone is interested in creating a bracket, they should talk to Chris West at Bazzaz to see what would be required. I'd offer my bike up as a guinea pig if needed.

It takes Bazzaz a couple of weeks to have the wiring harnesses fabricated, so this system should be available in a few weeks.

here is how to wire the Motty. It is not a plug and play story. You need to know electronic basics and understand the wiring. You will need to translate from German to English, let me know if you have dificulties.

Some important things to do upfront :

1. Sync your throttle bodies !!!
2. Disable with TuneEcu the Lambda probs
3. Remove and disable the complete SLS system, otherwise your Lambda readings are not correct
4. Disable gear restriction and remove secondary butterflies, not mandatory

Check your exhaust system for any leaks ! This will also mess up the Lambda readings if there are any.

Assembly steps :

1. Cut wiring harness into correct lenght, the Motty fits perfectly in the back of the bike
2. Most signals will come from the big grey throttle body connector at the right side of the bike, you need to cut the two injection wires and
tap the signal of the TPS sensor, you can you Posi Locks for testing
3. Tap Temp Signal from the sensor at the left side of the bike between the cylinders
4. Tap RPM and Speed signal behind the Mask
5. Mount the LC1 unit to grap the signal from the second Lambda probe and mount it where the snorkel airintake was sitting there is enough room
6. Calibrate lambda probs at free air and use the original position to fit them back in the bike
7. Mount all cable nicely
8. Fire it up
