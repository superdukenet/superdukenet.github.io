First off, welcome to the forum.

As for your questions:
-I myself haven't carried a pillion, but know a fair few members have. I believe the general consenseous is yes she'll be happy, as long as she is a medium/smaller lady and if it isn't for long journeys (guessing from what you have said, it won't be long ones). Others will be able to shed more light on this though.

-The engine is a very strong one. It's the same as they use in the Adventure . I'm thinking that the reason you don't see many high milage SD is because anyone who has one is keeping it to themselves as they know what a hoot it is.

-On new/unmodded bikes it's not uncommon for there to be a fair bit of snatchiness on low RPMs. Some bikes have never suffered it. There are a fair few mods to sort this out, some more effective than others. It has been known to be able to totally eradicate it. For more info you are better off using the search function as there have been many topics on this subject.

-'05+'06 models had sharper turning ability and a more "fiesty" engine. This is what caused a lot of press to call it a nutters bike. The styling isn't to everones taste.
'07 onwards had a restyle in the looks department, but also they softened the suspension a bit/changed the steering angle and retuned the engine slightly to try to sort out some of the criticizim about the earlier model.
In '08 they brought out the R which had a single seat, stiffer suspension and steeper steering angle. '09 on the R had the engine tuned to give about 12bhp more than the standard. (If you want to take out your missus, don't get the R)

-I think the only rather common failer is the water pump seal/shaft. This normally only effects older/high milage ones. Apart from that it's only the normal things on a bike to look out for.

-I see from your short list that you're going for a certain type of bike
There are a few ex-Triple riders amongst our mist, they would be the best ones to tell you about the comparison between an SD and S3.
With the MT, well , if you like it then thats your choice, but I've never seen one on the road.
The Tuono, i believe, is just like the SD, you either love it or you don't. I know a few people who own them and they won't swap them for anything, just like I wouldn't with my SD.

Your best bet is to go try out the bikes in question and see which you personally like to ride the most. But whatever your choice you make I hope it makes you happy in the long run.

Ive done about 60000 miles on superdukes. Mileage is no problem.

Some pillions have issues with exhaust fumes swhrling up behind the pillion in an updraught and making them nausious...have her smell for it on test ride. Also, no grab rails or handles....just you to grab on to mate.

Ive been out with an MT01 following my old slower SD amd he proper cooked his brakes with just a single rider. Avoid.!

As for the other two....depends on what you want....

Top speed, heavier with slightly less resale value and reliability (subjectively).

Slower steering, less aggressive looks, British tradition and Ace cafe friendly burger n chips appeal

Or fast, great handling, more unique looks, slightly poorer dealer network than Ape and much worse than Trumpet but an addictive package and smiles per mile.

SD really is a great bike - just makes you smile. It's generally well engineered and refined from front to back - form really does follow function. It doesn't look like a frickin hedgehog with bits sticking out of it like an after thought. Engines are strong if looked after. Two up - not too much padding but comfortable enough. My wife is 8.5 stone & 5' 8" & fits well. Yours - if she doesn't know already, will need to use her legs - grip your arse with her knees to stop pushing you into the tank - this will counter no grab rail. Compared to your gixxer it's not as smooth - power pulses and all that, but that'll be the same with any twin....and low speed throttle is on/off so feathering the throttle in town isn't particularly relaxed as you do need to work at it harder than on a four. I now ride with my 1st finger over the brake lever to steady my hand...which isn't a bad thing anyway. You do get used to dealing with it so don't let it put you off. All in all....it's brilliant !!
