#3
If she is serious about photography then she needs to try and get some work assisting. It's the way you learn your trade and I did it for nearly 8 years. Had some fantastic times and went all over the world. Shagged a fair few models (proably not what you want to here as it's your daughter ) and learnt a hell of a lot.
She could either try and get some work at a local studio. They aren't always the best payers and the hours can be long. Advantages are that you get to use the space after hours and if the owner is nice and likes you, the gear as well. The bonus though is that you get to work on shoots for photographers that use the studios. If they like you then that is your in" for working for them freelance away from the studio. Get 2 or 3 of these guys and you can leave the studio and go freelance. If you are a hard worker, pleasant to be around eager to learn and help out then you will do well. Your name gets passed about and things go well from there. I never got to work with any famous to Joe Public names, although I was offered Lord Snowdon. Having to call him Lord and take him breakfast in bed when on trips just isn't my thing at all, so I turned it down. Maybe that was stupid but hey it's done now. Friends who were a few years in front of my started off in Holborn Studios and got to work a lot with the likes of Bailey, Annie Leibovitz , Eve Arnold and Rankin to name a few Lucky fookers!

The other way is to just try and get in with photographers but without a in through someone that's extremely difficult. I was lucky that I had m8s who were photographers but without that it would have been a massive struggle. She could of course do the collage thing. One of my peers did it that route and said he learnt more in 1 weeks assisting than in 2 years at collage! Still a great grounding in the basics. Of course if she is really talented then she might just do it all on her own and sod the assisting route. I know people who have done it but its not easy.

One tip if she does start assisting is try and work with a talented still life guy at some point. These guys are anal as fook and can drive you insane trying to chase a highlight or shadow but their knowledge of lighting and planes of focus ect is usually hard to beat. You would be amazed how many hours can be spent lighting a watch

Im a hobby photographer but i know a coupleof pro guys/wedding photographers. There are a LOT of people out there who think they can do it but cant. If she is really good, she will need to learn the crafts mentioned above, lighting in particular if she goes int portrait/studio work imho.

The shots show someone who has an understanding of what they want but needs to develop the skills and techniques to get it. Learning how depth of field, aperture, light, exposure all combine to manipulate the image is the starting point. Refinement comes later and a style develops. There a few guys out there who run workshops (for a fee) for budding photogs on their specialist subjects (weddings or rock music or nature or whatever). These can also be good ways to get education, experience and also make contacts...another vital part of working life.

A basic DSLR with a couple of fixed lenses is a good place to start. Doesnt have to be expensive if you buy used kit thats well looked after. It can get very expensive though. Thousands for a decent lens is totally normal....and thats just SLR photography. Larger formats and flashy lighting kits etc really add up. Plus a decent Mac and software (ususally pirated) adds to the fun!
