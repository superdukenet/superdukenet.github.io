First - sorry about the pics; I used my camera phone.

I think this is a worthwhile swapover - the new looks much better than the old and suits the style of the bike.

The new style indicators are available from KTM dealers and are reasonably priced at just under a tenner each. There are two types - one for front right and rear left, the other for front left and rear right, although I couldn't see any differences.

Although they are a straight swap mechanically the wiring is slightly different between the old and new versions. I thought I'd post this guide as the dealer mentioned the wiring was different and made it sound more difficult than it was. It's also probably a much easier route than making new connections anywhere, and almost looks 'factory' to anyone else disassembling the bike in the future.

The key words in this work are 'carefully' and 'gently'.

The old versions are disconnected by unplugging two connectors on the back of the reflector inside the indicator, which is easily dismantled by unscrewing the small screw on the back and it pretty much all falls apart in your hand.

The new versions come with a short (about 6") length of wire ready attached, with a single plastic connector at the end. If you take the new style indicator apart you find the reflector doesn't come out quite so easily. It slides out forwards - you need to slip a small screwdriver behind the reflector at the wide end and 'gently' ease it out.

You'll find there are similar connectors to the old style inside, but one is much wider (the other is the same) so the wires won't swap directly over.

What I did was to snip the connectors off the wire attached to the new indicators and remove the wire completely. Then I took the unsheathed connector on the wire still attached to the bike and held it firmly on the crimped piece with a pair of pliers. I got a pointed electrical tester (a bradawl would work as well) and 'carefully' pushed it up the middle of the open end of the connector, slightly opening it wider. Then I got a pair of pointed nose pliers and did it again, opening the connector still further. With 'care' you can open the 05/06 connector up just enough for it to fit onto the wider connector on the 07 reflector. After that it's plain sailing, and in true Haynes fashion, reassembly is the reversal of removal! Just be 'careful' sliding the new reflector back into position.

One more thing - don't have a helpful 10 year old around to tell you that only one side is working when you test it. Last time I looked this bike doesn't have 4-way hazard lights!
