All racebikes (at least here in Northern California w. the AFM - the oldest road-racing club in the US) run water-wetter only, since coolant is sick as snot & hard to clean up. Think about the 250p example - 4-stroke 250cc ninjas running within 20% of redline , pegged continuously, for 4-hours (250 class AFM 4-hour endurance race on Kawasaki EX-250 racebikes - been done several times)

So for heat-dissipation there are no worries.

As for boiling point - I'm not certain how high it is, but it's definitely high enough. Why should it matter unless you lose system pressure through a leak or something? Cavitation will be impossible under pressure.

I can't speak to the corrosion-inhibition part of the equation, except anecdotally. In 15 years of running only water-wetter on everything, I have not had corrosion problems on any street-bikes, race-bikes, or cars.
