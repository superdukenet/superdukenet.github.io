#4
Ask a mate to give you a hand.
Lift the rear wheel up in the air to fully extend the shock. You can do this by balancing the bike on the front wheel and the side stand. Get your buddy to measure the distance from somewhere on the swing near the center of the wheel to somewhere straight above it (somewhere on the silencer).

Now put all your riding gear on. Get up on the bike and sit on it in riding position. Position the bike near a wall to slightly touch the wall with the steering bar end to stop you from tipping over. Now get your mate to measure between the exact same two points again.
Adjust your preload to get a difference of 30 - 40 mm compared to the first measure.

Once you've got this, measure the sag when the bike is standing up (no side stand, hold it it up straight) but without rider. Now the difference should be about 10 mm compared to the first measure.
If this is smaller than about 10 mm, you need a harder spring. If it's more than about 10 mm, you need a softer spring.

Don't forget to adjust the preload on the forks. It should be between 35 and 50 mm. It's done in asimilar manner.

Hi,

The 6mm of pre load mentioned on the label under the seat relates to the spring length not the length of threads that are visible.
To measure this you would need to loosen off the spring adjustment until the spring is free then measure the length of the spring and then tighten down the pre load adjustment until the spring is compressed by 6mm. (if loose spring measures 120mm tighten until it is 114mm).

Also Iâ€™m sure the static sag relates to the amount the forks or rear end drops under the weight of the bike only.
Rider sag relates to the amount the forks or rear end drops under the weight of the bike and rider.
