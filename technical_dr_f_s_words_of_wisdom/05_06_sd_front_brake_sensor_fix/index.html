Warning: This is a modification to a safety critical part of the bike. Use this info your own risk.



I have recently replaced my front brake sensor on my 2006 SD. The original front brake sensor is part no: 61011050000. The fault was a worn sensor pin. The barrel that holds the sensor pin was worn, and this allowed the pin to wobble. This caused the light to stay on whenever I lightly or slowly squeezed the lever. Instead of paying the $125AUD for a new part (local supplier quote), I made my own for $7AUD. I've discovered that some overseas suppliers will not deal with Australian customers. I tried to fix the unit, but the epoxy packaging was too thick, I accidently damaged it. So, I build my own sensor using a reed switch.

I brought a security reed switch from www.jaycar.com.au (Part No LA5070 $6.95AUD), cracked it open and removed the Reed switch. This switch is a normally closed (NC) type. I cut the normally open (NO) lead off, as it's not needed. I soldered the original cable into it and glued the reed switch using epoxy resin into the original epoxy package (see pic below). I glued a magnet onto a sensor land of the front brake lever using automotive Silicone. I replaced the brake light with an LED.



The EOM brake light used 1.8A at 13V. The LED light uses 50mA at 13V. I don't know the spec for my reed switch, but by looking at it, my guess is that is has a max rating of 100mA. The draw of the LED is within the limit of the reed switch.

The new sensor packaging is very well worked. The epoxy job is strong. The new sensor clips into the old sensors spot. The advantage of a reed switch is it is water proof and has few moving parts. It should last for a very long time.

The only weak part of my fix is I have siliconed the magnet to the sensor land of the brake lever. Silicon is best adhesive I can think of that is vibration resistant. Will the magnet hold in the vibration and wind force ?. I do not know without trial. However I'm not happy with this and would like to find a better solution ; so will figure out a better way to secure the magnet later. Watch this space

J

PS/ As a check, I pulled the magnet off to see how strong the silicon was. It's quite strong. I reglued. I am pretty confident it is stay. I will be checking it with my periodic light checks until a better solution is found.
