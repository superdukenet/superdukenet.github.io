#2
I've used both, and both do the job nicely. Personally I prefer the GPR due to the fact it easier to see and adjust with gloves while on the fly.
I think it looks better too. The ohlins / scotts does have some other adjustments for turning the bars lock to lock, but I never used them since the distance needed to turn the bars at speed is minimal. (They were originally designed for dirt bike and adapted to sport bikes).
Being able to see the damper might seem trivial, but when those cheeky fookers i call friends, turned mine all the way up and i nearly drop it trying to make a turn out of my pit., it made sense.

Plus - the GPR dial rotates 360 degress . IE if you're at 25, keep turning to get to zero. the Scott / ohlins must be turned all the way back around.

I have owned both. I liked the way both worked but I got rid of the GPR and kept the Scotts. The reason is that the GPR would leak after a year or so, sent it back to GPR and a year later, leak again. Contacted GPR, they tell me it's normal, the damper needs to be serviced regularly, seals need to be replaced every year or two. I decided this is bs. So I sent it back for complete service and sold it. Never had a problem with the Scotts (Ohlins).
