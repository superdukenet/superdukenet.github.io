I gotta ask - why does one care?

The "alternator" is on, as soon as the engine turns. But that's not all that determines charging.

Regardless, you aren't going to get much (if any) charge at idle, if that's what you're thinking (that is, charging the battery by just letting the bike idle). Not great for the valves either, idling for super long periods. The charging system - while functioning - isn't at peak output at idle. Don't believe me? Just watch the headlight brighten as you rev the engine a little. The regulator is what determines if there's enough juice (above a certain threshold) to actually charge. If your concern is whether you're going to run down the battery at idle (?), not going to happen either, presuming all elements of your charging/electrical system are functioning nominally. The charging system is designed to prevent battery rundown at idle.

In any case, you can pretty easily measure the output at idle versus higher revs ... I doubt its of any help, but the only KTM spec for the generator output is that its 450W at 6000 RPM. I'm going to guess that's the peak output RPM, and that its pretty much flat beyond that.
