With the cam timing, don't worry about locking the crank etc. With both cam covers off and plugs out (I used the plug spanner that comes in your toolkit as it was the only one I had that was small enough) You'll see 2 timing marks on opposite sides of each camwheel. You want to have these marks lined up horizontally meaning the camlobes will be facing outwards and with no load on your buckets. You can turn the engine over just on the starter to bring your marks into place. Then use your feeler gauges to measure your clearances, take your shims out and measure them with a set of verniers and work out what shims you need to order. Once the first cylinder is done re-assemble and repeat with the rear one.

I got away with leaving the oil tank in but pulled the radiator which was ok as I was doing coolant, oil, filters as well anyway. I hope this helps.

Update:

have spent a coupla days on this now, largely because I discovered i need a tool I didn't have and also cos I got a late start on Saturday (not my fault!).

Anyway, the bike is totally in bits, engine all ripped open etc. I've got the rocker covers off, locked TDC on both cylinders seperately to measure the valve clearances. Have whipped out the cams, buckets and shims to measure them so I can see what size teh new shims need to be to bring it all back into tolerance. 2 of my clearances are within tolerance already so I only need 6 shims, none of them are crazily out but they need doing to be right.

I'll be calling around my regular KTM dealers tomorrow for stock and whoever has them all in will be seeing my in the car with cash in hand. Once that's done, the whole thing gets reassembled, oil filled and we're back on the road! (fingers crossed!!!)

I'm learning a LOT as I go but I hope it will all be worth it.

I've already got some more jobs to tackle: Balance throttle bodies, remove and fit new chain n sprox (not due for 10k though).
