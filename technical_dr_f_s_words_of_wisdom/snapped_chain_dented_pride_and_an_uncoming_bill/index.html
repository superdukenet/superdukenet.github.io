Well today was a bit of an adventure that's for sure.

Brought the Superduke out from winter hibernation, bit of tweaking, adjusting, sorting etc and away we went...

Was riding with B.. and having fun... the bike was superb... The roads all good too. The Superduke makes a cracking noise... Although i must admit it's a handful to hang onto over 110mph.. i may need to get used to that.

So i'm riding along and getting through the gears when suddenly i get a VERY weird noise and lose all drive...

Whip the clutch in and pull to a halt.. my chain is no longer on the bike...

To compound my fears i have oil pissing out on the floor !!!!!

As you can imagine, by this time i'm not a happy bunny at all.

40 mins later and the bike is on a recovery truck being dragged back home.. i then survey the damage and it's better than i expected.

Superdukes seem to have a built in engine case saver, which has seemingly saved my engine casing

Sadly it's bent and snapped one of the mounting bolts which will need extracting/drilling, but it could have been a lot worse.

It's also bent the clutch push rod by the looks of it, but again, not the end of the world as they won't be much money.

I've got a tiny nick in the casing, but it's a protruding part, not a hole, so not worried by that at all.

So, apart from bill for a new chain, sprockets, push rod and engine protector i'm pretty much good to go... Not a complete disaster in all honesty.

Could have easily ended up lying on the floor with broken bones as the chain wrapped itself up, or a shattered bike etc.. So... not a terrible result.

Supersprox ... yes, steel outer. For anyone here - regardless the tooth count, metal/alloy type used - and I can't emphasize this enough - always, always, ALWAYS replace chains and both front and rear sprockets as a set. Beyond personal safety, and if for no other reason, to avoid wasting money on premature wear of new components.

Now you're going to have 50 posts on how guys swap individual components in and out, who've never had an "issue". And maybe for balance, we should post up the x-rays of some of the guys where things didn't turn out so well.

As DIDs are not all created equally, brand of chain should have nothing to do with the cause of this failure. Chain model and chain/sprocket condition (plate elongation, roller wear, sprockets' teeth and saddles wear), chain tension, and any mismatch of component state of wear - may have had more than a little something to do with it.

11K - not much ... How many miles on the chain before the rear sprocket swap Weeksy?

For thos who don't know, because of the layout of the swingarm, frame , motor, the chain will get tighter as the swingarm goes thru its motion when more weight is put on to the bike. If you do not adjust your chain at it's tightest point, and you have the chain too tight you will over stress the chain or bearings. Red rust on my chains = chain game over. Rust on the links will destory the "O" rings in no time. The "O" rings keep the factory lube inside the chain. The key is to keep the "O" rings happy, and the factory lub inside the chain. Moly lub on the output splines will keep the rust off the shaft longer than no lube.
