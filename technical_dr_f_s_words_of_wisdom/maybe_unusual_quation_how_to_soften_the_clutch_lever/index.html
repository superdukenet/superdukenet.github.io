I got a small question regarding the clutch lever operation.

Background:
A few months ago I got into an accident when a car "jumped" infront of me, and I run into its side.
Beside the damage to the bike (lucky for me, I hit him at "just" 20 km/h after trying to break), I broke my left hand wrist.

Present day:
At the moment, I still have some issues with strength and movement of my wrist. Still doing physical theraphy.

I'm trying to go back to riding, but operating the clutch lever force me to use excessive force from my arm to counter the too low strength of my grip.
This cause me to put too much weight on the handle-bars, and so give me issues in operating the bike and movement.

Since the clutch is hydraulic, beside moving the lever a bit more away from the grip handle, is there a way to soften it up a bit, so I will not need to use too much power to operate it?

And yeah, I know the "so drive without a clutch you sissy!" response I get from my mates answer , but it doesn't help me right now when I drive sometimes in town (and since I'm selling my car, my bike will be my only transport for a week or so).

#3
After market dog leg design levers, (not shorty), often make the action easier that the standard levers. Other than that there isn't an easier way to make clutch lighter. I have an Oberon and it's pretty much the same as standard. Also try using the end of the levers for more leverage rather than the index and third finger. Good luck.
