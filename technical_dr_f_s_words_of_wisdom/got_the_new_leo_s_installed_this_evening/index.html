Okay, I got the Leo's installed this evening after work. Not to difficult although still took me more time than I wanted (always does when I do moto mechanics it seems). For starters they sound kick ass. I can't wait to get out for a full ride. As it was I couldn't resist testing them around the block. I'm sure the neighbors loved me as it was midnight.

The only problem I saw was not with the pipes but with the subframe on the SD. On the left/clutch side their was a strip of welding running down the inner tube of the subframe. This prevented me from being able to fit the spacer in the subframe to anchor the can. The other side of the subframes tube was smooth on the inside so no problem. I think it will be okay or I can probalby come up with my own rig to sturdy that side up.

Also, how air tight does the system need to be? I have it to the point where I can not feel any air blowing out at all where the various pieces of the exhuast connect together. But just because I can't feel it doesn't mean some isn't blowing out/sucking in. What's the opinion on that?

Edit: Oh yeah. Forgot to say. My god the stock exhaust is heavy! I mean I read it here but nothing like picking that thing up and feeling it for yourself.

I had bout the same problem as you did with the installation, if you look closely at the cans from the rear they will more than likely not be perfect.

Perfection is a state of mind more than any other 1 thing to me, I personally do not partake in absolute perfection, (already crazy! why add insult to injury)

the seal on the leos is a good fit, you want it to be "unloaded" not to be in a bind, there is really nothing you can do to have an absolute perfect seal.

if it were to have a small exhaust leak, it will not hurt a thing that this dude could think of, maybe a little extra popping on deceleration, thats about it.

I do not cover my exhaust ports when running to test this system for leaks, I am worried about back pressure and the results of doing this to test it.
