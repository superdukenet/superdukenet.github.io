+1 what he said = "personally I would prefer weaker over stronger."

You can get away with a lot of brake force with a heavy bike, like a Harley, but with the lite Super Duke, I don't suggest it.

You need to use the front brake for most of the braking.

The weight transfers to the front, the rear wheel will unload, and it takes very little braking force to lock the rear wheel up = a high side crash.

Try flushing the rear brake system first and adjusting the pedal to fit your body position.

The SD has fan funkin tastic front brakes...use them for 90% of your braking, or more.

I don't use a lot of rear brake most of the time, but our driveway at home is fairly steep (probably in the region of 25-30°incline up to the garage) and whilst manoeuvering bikes around on the drive I needed to hold the SD steady before riding it back into the garage so I put my foot on the rear brake pedal and the bike started rolling backwards down the drive! There's plenty of pedal pressure so it isn't full of air, but I was somewhat concerned about the excessive amount of force that I had to apply to just stop the bike rolling, way above what's required on the other bikes (Tiger 800, ZX-10R track bike, WR250).
Another question mark against KTM's Engineering capabilities, alongside the expanding fuel tanks and the 1290 rear hub bearing issues... This one looks like a mis-match between piston sizes in the master cylinder and the caliper, not enough attention paid to it in the product development stages.
The SMT rear caliper looks like a good solution, I may look for one of those as a Winter project.
