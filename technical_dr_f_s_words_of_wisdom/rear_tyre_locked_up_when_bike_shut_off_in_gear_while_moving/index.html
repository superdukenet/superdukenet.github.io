Rear tyre locked up when bike shut off in gear while moving

Jerryn206 Forum member Topic author

#1
I was on the freeway with a group of friends when my buddy in front of me downshifted a few gears, unexpectedly, to prepare to wheelie so I grabbed a ton of brake and the bike shut off (not sure why) at around 60mph. As soon as it shut off, the rear tyre locked up until I skidded to a stop. Is this normal? I've seen my friends hit their killswitch while riding and their bikes keep rolling just fine. Is this just a superduke thing or what? Anyone else experienced this? It was rather scary as I've never had this happen on any bike and I panicked because I wasn't sure what happened. I suppose pulling in the clutch would have solved the issue but you'd think there would be some kind of built in safety mechanism that would keep the tyre from locking up in gear if the bike shuts off while moving.

Post 2 by Colonel_Klinck, 25 Apr 2013, 17:28

#2
If you were very low on gas the bike will stall on very heavy braking due to the fuel moving to the front of the tank. With the rear end light, the engine stalled and no slipper clutch fitted the tyre could just skid along. If you've ever tried to bump start a superduke you'll see how difficult it is to get the engine turning over using the rear wheel.

I take it she fired straight up again?

Crotchrockety Forum member

Post 4 by Crotchrockety, 25 Apr 2013, 17:51

#4
Yeah, I had my bike cut out and lock up too. Mine lost spark due to a faulty reg / rectifier, but had the same result. Skidding until I pulled the clutch in and coasted.

As Klink said, the SD does not have a slipper, aka anti-hopping clutch, as standard. Without the slipper clutch, when the bike stalls, the built up compression in the cylinders locks the back wheel. If the bike is pointed in a straight line, you can pull the clutch in to unlock the rear wheel. (Note: If its out of alignment, you risk unanticipated flying lessons from a high side crash, so be careful).
