The Corbin saddled I ordered for my Superduke arrived today. It was a simple installation, maybe 15 minutes. Grabbed a few hi-rez photos and posted them here:
I think it looks decent, but I like the looks of the stock saddle quite well. The stocker was also plenty comfortable (to me) for short rides. I didn't make it down to the Monterey peninsula this morning. Just as well.

Turns out the new Corbin saddle didn't suit me at all. I'm going to return it. It's got edges on the sides at the very forward end which stick out further than the stock seat and these edges of the saddle poked my inner thigh about mid way up from my knees. I ride with a tight grip on the bike at the knees. It didn't take long at all for these two leading edges to become uncomfortable. That turned into mild pain a bit later. I added photos to show how the very forward edge of the seat is longer and the frontal side edges stick out more than the stock seat. This may not bother some riders at all. I'm pretty sure my riding style is a factor, and my height may be a factor as well (I'm 5'6"). If you ride with your knees out in the breeze, this issue won't affect you at all. If you grip the bike lightly at the knees, it may be a minor issue. The actual seating surface was just fine. But if you hold the bike tightly with your knees, you may want to look at another saddle -- or at least confirm the return policy of your accessories dealer before buying this saddle. As always, "your own mileage may vary."

If anyone wants it, let me know. I'll wait a week before I return it. I paid $250 plus tax & shipping. It's yours for $240, shipped anywhere in the lower 48 states.

It may not be a problem for you. But it wasn't something I could get used to. The stock seat may look like it has an even sharper edge in this area but I can't feel that edge at all. It just doesn't stick out like the Corbin does. The plastic base which either seat bolts onto gets a little wider as you move forward and the Corbin seat extends further forward (so that forward edge sticks out further). You can see that in the last images where I stacked the two seats. The Corbin is aligned correctly, resting over the stock seat. You can tell it's aligned correctly because of the 1 inch gap to the tank, as well as the fact that it couldn't go any further backwards because it would cover the key hole which releases the plastic seat base (if moved further back).

I also circled the only problem with the stock seat which I was trying to get rid of.

But that edge only bothers me at a stop. Being short in stature, my legs have to be straight to touch ground so that edge presses into the top of my inner thigh. Taller riders would have a slight, natural bow in the leg when stopped and likely wouldn't press on that edge.
But I have no complaints about the stock seat while I'm in motion, so it's the one I'll stay with.
